Do the "before you fly" items at home, on home Wi-Fi, with your Australian SIM working. Several of them need an SMS code, and many apps and websites can't be downloaded once you're in China.

1. Visa: not needed, with one date to check

  • Australian passport holders can enter China visa-free for up to 30 days per entry for tourism. Two weeks is well within that.
The catch: the visa-free scheme is officially confirmed only until 31 December 2026. As of 20 November 2026 China hadn't announced an extension into 2027. It has renewed every year so far, each time in November. If you arrive in 2027, check the Chinese Embassy website for the extension notice before you fly. If it hasn't come through, you'd need a tourist (L) visa.
  • Passport: at least 6 months' validity left and a couple of blank pages.
  • Carry a printed or saved copy of your return flight and your first hotel booking in case immigration asks.
  • Arrival card: fill it in online before you fly. It's free at s.nia.gov.cn/ArrivalCardFillingPC/, in the "NIA 12367" app, or in the Alipay mini-program. Save the QR code. Avoid any site that charges for it. Paper cards and airport kiosks are still available as a fallback.

2. Phone and internet: the most important part

On any Chinese network (hotel Wi-Fi, café Wi-Fi, a Chinese SIM) these are blocked: Google (Search, Maps, Gmail, Drive, Translate, Play Store), WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, ChatGPT and most Western news sites.

The fix is a data connection that routes outside China. You then don't need a VPN.

  • Option A: a travel eSIM (Airalo, Holafly, Nomad, Saily, Trip.com and similar). Pick a mainland China plan that says Google/WhatsApp work. Install it at home and switch it on when you land. To check your Samsung supports eSIM: Settings › Connections › SIM manager › "Add eSIM".
  • Option B: Australian roaming (Telstra, Optus or Vodafone international day pass). It's dearer but needs no setup, and everything works because your traffic goes through Australia.
  • VPN: backup only, for hotel Wi-Fi. Install, pay for and test it in Australia, because VPN sites and app downloads are blocked inside China. VPNs got less reliable there in 2026.

Samsung-specific:

  • The Google Play Store won't load in China. Install every app before you leave.
  • Samsung Wallet and Google Pay don't work for payments there. Use Alipay.
  • Keep your Australian SIM active for SMS (bank and Alipay codes). If you use an eSIM for data, turn data roaming off on the Australian SIM so you don't get a bill.
  • Public Wi-Fi often needs a Chinese phone number, which is one more reason to rely on mobile data.

3. How to pay

China is almost cashless. People pay by scanning QR codes everywhere, even at street stalls.

  • Alipay is your main tool. Sign up with your Australian mobile number, verify your passport and link a Visa or Mastercard. Visa is reported to be the most reliable. Make a small test payment before you depend on it.
  • WeChat: install it too. It works as a backup wallet with foreign cards, and it's how locals, guides and hotels will message you.
  • Link two different cards in case one gets declined, and tell both banks you're travelling.
  • Fees: small payments are generally free, but about 3% has applied above ¥200. Check in the app. A no-foreign-fee travel card helps.
  • Cards directly: fine at big hotels, malls and chains, rarely anywhere small.
  • Cash: carry ¥500–1,000 in small notes as a backup. Airport and Bank of China ATMs take Australian cards.

4. Maps and getting around

  • Don't rely on Google Maps, even with an eSIM. Its data is often out of date, and China's shifted coordinate system can put your blue dot hundreds of metres from where you really are.
  • Use Amap (Gaode Maps, 高德地图). It has English, live traffic and proper public-transport routing.
  • Save every address in Chinese characters. Paste them into Amap or DiDi, and show them to drivers.
  • Taxis: DiDi. Use the app or the DiDi mini-program inside Alipay, which is in English and pays automatically. Avoid touts at airports and stations.
  • Metro: scan the transport QR code in Alipay at the gate (Alipay › Transport).
  • Trains: your passport is your ticket. Book on Trip.com or 12306 (both in English). Check which station, because big cities have several (Beijing South, Beijing West, Shanghai Hongqiao…). Allow 45–60 minutes for passport and security checks.

5. Apps to install before flying

  • Alipay: payments, DiDi, metro, translator
  • WeChat: messaging, backup payments
  • Amap: maps
  • DiDi: taxis (optional if you use it via Alipay)
  • Trip.com: trains, flights, hotels
  • Google Translate with the Chinese offline pack downloaded (or Microsoft Translator)
  • Your eSIM app, plus a VPN if you've decided on one
  • Save offline copies of bookings, tickets and a passport scan on the phone itself, not only in Gmail or Google Drive

6. Passport and registration

  • Carry your passport at all times. You need it for trains, hotels, museums and many attractions.
  • Big attractions (Forbidden City etc.) are booked against your passport number, so the name and number on your bookings must exactly match your passport.
  • Hotels register you with the police automatically. If you stay in a private home, you must register at the local police station within 24 hours.

7. Practical bits

  • Power: 220V. Australian plugs fit most Chinese wall sockets, so you usually don't need an adapter.
  • Emergency numbers:
Police 110Ambulance 120Fire 119
  • Australian help: Embassy in Beijing, Consulate-General in Shanghai, and DFAT's 24-hour Consular Emergency Centre on +61 2 6261 3305. Register your trip on Smartraveller.
  • Get travel insurance and keep the policy saved offline.
  • Keep medication in its original packaging with a copy of the prescription.
  • Carry tissues and hand sanitiser. Many toilets don't supply paper.
