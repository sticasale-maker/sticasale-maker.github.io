Where the hidden savings are

  • Skip Reno. Pick up a car at San Francisco airport, drive to Tahoe and back (about 3.5 h each way) and return it there before the flight to Venice: no extra flight and no one-way fee.

  • Turkish Airlines' round-the-world fare for the late-summer trip. It goes from Australia via North America and Istanbul to Europe, and back via Istanbul and Asia, with free stopovers: the late-summer shape (Sydney → San Francisco, stop, → Istanbul → Venice; Venice → Istanbul → Singapore → Sydney). Australian Frequent Flyer's sample of that shape cost 2,105 in economy and 7,862 in business in 2024. It's sold through agents only and prices may have moved, so get a quote before buying the late-summer legs.

  • Mix airlines in business: TAP via Lisbon, San Francisco → Venice, is 3,606 against 5,306 on United.

  • Book the bag with the low-cost hops (Ryanair Genoa → Catania, Catania → Malta, Malta → Bari, Bari → Athens and Chania → Venice); it costs more at the airport.

  • Leave buffers on separate tickets. Bags don't transfer between them, so allow at least three hours, or a night, on self-made connections.

Checked and not worth it

  • A return starting in Italy to cover both the June flight home and the flight back out: about 3,000, more than two one-ways.

  • One round-the-world ticket for both trips: they now run in opposite directions.

  • Hidden-city tricks: they put the later legs and checked bags at risk.

When to book

  • Now: the spring long-haul as one multi-city ticket each (Sydney → Tokyo, Tokyo → Prague, Venice → Sydney), which usually prices well below separate one-ways; changeable fares suit a first long trip together. Hold the late-summer flights until after spring.

  • Two to four months out: Ryanair, Aegean, the Liberty Lines hydrofoils once the spring timetable is out around February, and the Puglia and Crete hire cars.

  • Also early: Annecy, Turin, Camogli, Salina, Stromboli, Valletta, Gozo, the Puglia masseria, Matera, Loutro, Kobarid, Bohinj, Ljubljana and Venice. Book the second half, from Crete on, with free cancellation.

To discuss with Kelly

The three days where the connections are tight, each with the way to loosen it. Everything else in the itinerary has slack built in.

  1. Tue 4 May, Camogli → Genoa airport → Catania → Milazzo → Salina. Four connections in one day. The morning works on paper (train by 8:00, Volabus, bag drop for the 10:15 closes at 9:35) but one late regional train breaks it; a taxi from Camogli straight to the airport is about 40 minutes and €70. The afternoon is the unknown: the coach reaches Milazzo at 15:45, and whether a hydrofoil still leaves for Salina after that is the question for the 2027 timetable, due from Liberty Lines at the end of 2026. If none does, the fix is a night in Milazzo or Lipari, not a later flight.

  2. Sun 9 May, Stromboli → Milazzo → Catania → Malta. One hydrofoil, one coach, one flight, nothing behind them. The 07:15 boat gives four hours of slack; if the wind cancels it, the 11:50 only works with a taxi from Milazzo (about 1h45) instead of the coach, and bag drop for the 17:20 is close. This day decides whether Salina should come last instead of Stromboli.

  3. Fri 14 May, Gozo → Malta airport → Bari. The 10:35 flight means the 6:45 ferry and a taxi across Malta, so up at 5:30 on the last Gozo morning. Not fragile, just early. The alternative is three nights on Gozo and the last night near the airport.
