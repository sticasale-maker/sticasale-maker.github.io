  • Leave counts in working days. Marco flies out on Tue 13 Apr after work; Wed 14 Apr to Thu 27 May is 31 working days, with Mon 26 Apr a NSW public holiday (the extra Anzac Day holiday). Fly home from Chania on Wed 26 May, land in Sydney Thu 27 May, back at work Fri 28 May.

  • Kelly would rather avoid long taxi runs; driving yourselves in a hire car is fine. Trains, hydrofoils or flights cover the long hops.

  • Passports: Marco Italian and Australian, Kelly Australian and US. Marco travels in Europe on his Italian passport, so the EU's coming ETIAS authorisation doesn't apply to him; Kelly will need it on either of her passports once it starts, and there's no start date yet, with reporting pointing to sometime in 2027.

Where the hidden savings are

  • Skip Reno. Pick up a car at San Francisco airport, drive to Tahoe and back (about 3.5 h each way) and return it there before the flight to Venice: no extra flight and no one-way fee.

  • Turkish Airlines' round-the-world fare for the late-summer trip. It goes from Australia via North America and Istanbul to Europe, and back via Istanbul and Asia, with free stopovers: the late-summer shape (Sydney → San Francisco, stop, → Istanbul → Venice; Venice → Istanbul → Singapore → Sydney). Australian Frequent Flyer's sample of that shape cost 2,105 in economy and 7,862 in business in 2024. It's sold through agents only and prices may have moved, so get a quote before buying the late-summer legs.

  • Mix airlines in business: TAP via Lisbon, San Francisco → Venice, is 3,606 against 5,306 on United.

  • Book the bag with the low-cost hops (Ryanair Genoa → Catania, Catania → Malta, Malta → Bari, Bari → Athens); it costs more at the airport.

  • Leave buffers on separate tickets. Bags don't transfer between them, so allow at least three hours, or a night, on self-made connections.

Checked and not worth it

  • A return starting in Italy to cover both the June flight home and the flight back out: about 3,000, more than two one-ways.

  • One round-the-world ticket for both trips: they now run in opposite directions.

  • Hidden-city tricks: they put the later legs and checked bags at risk.

When to book

  • Now: the spring long-haul as one multi-city ticket each (Sydney → Tokyo, Tokyo → Geneva, Chania → Sydney), which usually prices well below separate one-ways; changeable fares suit a first long trip together. Hold the late-summer flights until after spring.

  • Two to four months out: Ryanair, Aegean, the Liberty Lines hydrofoils once the spring timetable is out around February, and the Puglia and Crete hire cars.

  • Also early: Annecy, Turin, Camogli, Genoa, Salina, Stromboli, Valletta, Gozo, the Puglia masseria, Matera and Loutro. Book the second half, from Crete on, with free cancellation.

The three days where the connections are tight, each with the way to loosen it. Everything else in the itinerary has slack built in.

  1. Tue 4 May, Genoa → Catania → Milazzo → Salina. Three connections in one day. With the night in Genoa the morning is easy: the Volabus from the city centre, about 25 min, for the 10:15 (bag drop closes at 9:35). The afternoon is the unknown: the coach reaches Milazzo at 15:45, and whether a hydrofoil still leaves for Salina after that is the question for the 2027 timetable, due from Liberty Lines at the end of 2026. If none does, the fix is a night in Milazzo or Lipari, not a later flight.

  2. Sun 9 May, Stromboli → Milazzo → Catania → Malta. One hydrofoil, one coach, one flight, nothing behind them. The 07:15 boat gives four hours of slack; if the wind cancels it, the 11:50 only works with a taxi from Milazzo (about 1h45) instead of the coach, and bag drop for the 17:20 is close. This day decides whether Salina should come last instead of Stromboli.

  3. Fri 14 May, Gozo → Malta airport → Bari. The 10:35 flight is gone. The 06:10 needs the last night near Malta airport; the 21:30 keeps a last full day on Gozo but lands in Bari at 22:50. To decide. Thu 20 May has the same problem: Bari → Athens moved to 05:50.
